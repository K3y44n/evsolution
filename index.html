The problem

Millions of EVs. One rush hour.

When drivers get home from work and plug in around 6:00 PM, charging demand lands on top of the evening peak. To cover the surge, utilities often start up gas or coal peaker plants: expensive, high-emission, and the last thing an EV should run on.

6 PM

Everyone arrives home and plugs in at once, stacking EV load on the evening peak.

Peakers

Dirty, costly plants start up just to cover a few hours of overload.

Cars sit idle

Most cars are parked for 12+ hours, so they don't need to charge right now.

The solution

Balance the grid with software, not steel.

GridSync is a Virtual Power Plant platform. It connects to vehicle telemetry APIs and home chargers, pauses or slows charging at peak times, and ramps it up in the small hours when wind power is plentiful and cheap. Drivers still wake up to a full battery.

FAQ

Good questions.

Will my car be full in the morning?

Yes. The optimizer always works backwards from your departure time, so the battery is full when you leave. If the window is too short, you're told up front.

What if I need a fast charge right now?

Tap override and charging starts immediately at full power. Smart charging is a default, never a lock.

Do I need new hardware?

No. GridSync talks to car and charger cloud APIs, so it works with equipment drivers already own.

What data does it use?

Only what scheduling needs: battery level, plug-in status, charger power and your departure time.

The platform

Four steps, zero new power lines.

1

Connect

Cloud APIs read battery level and plug-in status from the car, and charger APIs control the home charger.

2

Forecast

The platform predicts grid load, solar and wind output and energy prices for the next 24 hours.

3

Optimize

An algorithm fills the overnight demand valley first, while guaranteeing every car is full by the driver's departure time.

4

Dispatch

Commands pause or slow chargers during peak hours, or when regional solar drops, and ramp them up at 2:00 AM when wind is plentiful.
